Dr. Kirsty Feijen

Hello, and welcome to my personal page. I am an astrophysicist currently working as a post-doctoral researcher at the AstroParticle and Cosmology laboratory (APC) in Paris, France. My research focuses on very-high-energy gamma-ray astronomy within our own Galaxy. Particularly, I focus on the class of unidentified TeV gamma-ray sources whose origins remain unknown. My expertise includes data analysis and interpretation, development of models and programming. I use my knowledge in astrophysics, mathematics and general science to enthuse the wider community about science.

I grew up under the dark and clear Australian skies where I developed a fascination with the night sky that inspired my career path. I did my undergraduate and graduate studies at the University of Adelaide, completing my PhD in 2022 under the supervision of Prof. Gavin Rowell and Dr. Sabrina Einecke. During this time, I studied the very interesting and mysterious TeV gamma-ray source; HESS J1804-216. Combined observations from the interstellar medium with gamma-rays to model the region and ultimately gain an understanding of the origin of the emission.

I moved to Paris in 2023 to start my post-doctorate position at APC with CNRS, where my research aims to understand the particle acceleration processes occurring in extreme astrophysical environments in our own Galaxy. This involves looking at the objects linked to the violent death of stars, such as supernova remnants and pulsar wind nebulae. By combining observations from multiple gamma-ray observatories with the latest analysis methods, we can improve our understanding of the Milky Way.

My research to date has allowed me to work as part of multiple collaborations, including Mopra, H.E.S.S. and CTAO. Beyond research, I am passionate about promoting supportive and inclusive research environments, especially for early-career researchers and women in science.
